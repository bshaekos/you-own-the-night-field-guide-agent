# The Field Guide — Reorganized by Heading

*Source protocol for the Night Plan interview. This is the file `AGENTS.md` and the starter prompts point to — read it before running any heading.*

## What the Night Plan Is

Each chapter of *Who Owns the Night* closes with four questions, always in the same order: an **Audit** of where you are, the **Cost** of staying there, a **Swap** to try this week, and a **Check**, seven days later, on whether it worked.

Answered honestly and gathered under six headings, they become a working document — a self-portrait specific enough to hand to "any capable assistant" and ask it to help hold the line.

## The Six Headings

### 1 · Current State
*What your nights look like, when you're most vulnerable, what you're drowning out.*

Drawn from: **The Best Part of the Day**, **The Engineer Who Couldn't Stop**, **Architecture**.

- Walk through your current night, hour by hour — where is your own twelve-second window, the moment you stop choosing and start being chosen for?
- Which apps pull hardest, and when — what time, what mood?
- If you added ninety seconds of friction to your most-used app, what would come back?
- Design your ideal night across its four phases — coming-down, the part that's yours, powering-off, sleep. Which phase, fixed, would change the most?

### 2 · Underlying Needs
*What you're really after when you default to a screen, and who your quiet returns you to.*

Drawn from: **The Loneliness Algorithm**, **The Widower Who Kept the Light On**.

- When you reach for the phone at night, what are you actually looking for — distraction, reassurance, news, connection?
- Of your evening habits, which feed you and which only fill the time?
- What is your nervous system trying to drown out in the evening? Name the thing you don't want to sit with.
- Who, living or dead, does your quiet return you to — and who do you lose when the television is on?

*This heading can surface grief or old memories. See `AGENTS.md` → "Handling the harder material."*

### 3 · What Has Worked
*What felt right, what held even briefly, what doesn't tolerate half-attention.*

Drawn from: **The Remedies That Don't Work**.

- What have you already tried? What worked, even briefly, and what made it stop?
- Name one rule you've set that doesn't hold — what is it actually trying to protect?

### 4 · The Target
*What your nights should serve, your ideal night, the dormant capacity you want to move.*

Drawn from: **The Crack in the Wall**, **The Protected Hours**, **The Couple Who Came Back**.

- What would you protect, if you could — one evening activity you'd fight to keep?
- What, for you, does not tolerate half-attention? When did you last give it a full night?
- What work — creative, relational, professional — should your nights be serving?
- What dormant capacity would begin to move if the nights opened up? Name it, even if it feels too hopeful.

### 5 · Rules of Engagement
*Three non-negotiables, three defaults to stop, your nine-o'clock question, your three restart moves.*

Drawn from: **The Tool That Works for You** — already written, in the book, as a briefing to an assistant.

- What would you want an assistant to know about you to help protect your nights? Describe who you want to be from seven to midnight.
- What are your three non-negotiables, and the three things you want to stop doing by default?
- Write the one question you'd want asked at nine p.m. every night.
- Your three restart moves — see [`three-move-restart.md`](three-move-restart.md) for the protocol. Write them now, before you need them.

### 6 · The Long Game
*What sustained looks like a year out, the share of nights you mean to own, what you hope they produce.*

Drawn from: **The Long Game**.

- What would *sustained* look like a year from now? Describe a typical Tuesday night.
- What share of your evenings do you own now? What share do you want?
- In a week of real or simulated disruption, did three moves bring you back? Confirm Rules of Engagement already holds three restart moves before closing this section.

## Worked Example — Diane's Completed Night Plan

*An early reader, fifty-four, a hospital administrator in Ohio. Notice what it is not: not impressive, not literary, not optimized. It is specific, and specific is the entire job. Use it to calibrate register, never as a template to fill in.*

**CURRENT STATE.** I'm on the couch by 8:40 most nights with my phone and a glass of wine, and the news, and then the comments under the news. Worst nights are after days with conflict in them — a hard meeting, a complaint, anything with raised voices. Those nights I scroll like I'm looking for something, and I am, but it's not in the phone. What I'm drowning out: the replaying. I re-argue the day in my head and the phone turns the volume down on it.

**UNDERLYING NEEDS.** What I actually want at 8:40 is to feel off duty. Not entertained — released. The quiet returns me to my mother, who died three years ago and who I mostly only miss at night, and to my own thinking, which I used to like. I'd forgotten I used to like it.

**WHAT HAS WORKED.** The garden, two summers ago, until the weather ended it and I never restarted. Sunday calls with my sister — ninety minutes, never once regretted. Anything with my hands. Quilting, years back. What all of it has in common: I can't hold a phone while doing it, and I sleep afterward.

**THE TARGET.** Nights that end the day instead of leaking out of it. My ideal Tuesday: dinner, twenty minutes outside or with the quilt frame, call or read, lights lowering by ten. Who I want to be from seven to midnight: a woman who is finished, not a woman who is monitoring. The dormant thing I want moving again: the quilt. It's for my granddaughter. It's been a good intention for four years.

**RULES OF ENGAGEMENT.** Non-negotiables: phone charges in the kitchen at nine; Sunday is my sister's; the quilt frame stays set up in the den — visible, threaded, no putting it away. Stop-doing defaults: news after eight, comments ever, wine as the signal that the night has started. My nine o'clock question: *"Are you finishing the day, or re-arguing it?"* Restart moves, for after the bad stretches: kitchen charger, ten minutes at the frame, lights down at ten. Just those three. No apology tour.

**THE LONG GAME.** A year from now, sustained looks like five nights out of seven that I chose — not perfect nights, chosen ones. I currently own maybe two. What I hope the nights produce: the quilt, finished, on my granddaughter's bed, and a woman who goes up the stairs at ten-thirty with the day actually behind her.
