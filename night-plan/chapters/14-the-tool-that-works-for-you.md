## Summary

- James Chen is back — the engineer from Chapter Three, the one who moved his charger to the kitchen. He'd started using an AI assistant to help run his nights, sheepishly aware of exactly how that sounded from a man who'd spent six years building the very systems this book is about.
- His first attempt was a disaster, on purpose — he set the thing up to police him, and lasted four days before he hated it enough to quit. What worked was the opposite instruction: not to scold, but to reflect back what he'd told it, on a clearer day, he actually wanted. His own line: it's not a nanny, it's a mirror.
- The chapter is direct, more than almost anywhere else in the book, about what a tool like this can't do. It can't want things for the reader. It can't enforce. It can't substitute for other people, and it won't rescue an architecture that doesn't already exist. For anyone using this project to work through the Field Guide with an assistant, this is the chapter the whole exercise is built on — worth reading in full, not just the questions at the end of it.
