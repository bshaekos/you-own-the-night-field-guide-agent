## Summary

- Robert Hale didn't set out to reclaim his nights. He set out to learn to cook, at fifty-two, recently divorced, standing in front of a refrigerator he had no idea what to do with. His daughter's first phone call about it — "Dad, please don't kill yourself with onions" — did more for him than any article on attention ever could have.
- What happened over six months wasn't dramatic, but it illustrates a principle worth really absorbing: the night gets reclaimed by introducing an activity that simply does not tolerate half-attention. You cannot chop an onion and scroll at the same time. These things exclude the phone not by rule, but by nature.
- The chapter states plainly what the book has been circling: addition over subtraction. Find the thing. Let it in. Let it grow. What grew around Robert's stove, and where he is now, is the chapter's to tell.
