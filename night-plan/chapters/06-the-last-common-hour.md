## Summary

- Dinner at the Brennan house lasted eleven minutes. Paul Brennan knew this because he'd timed it, secretly, with the phone he wasn't supposed to notice everyone else using. Four people, four screens, a full house gone quiet in a way no earlier century ever produced: everyone home, everyone gone.
- The Brennans close out Part One as the only story in that stretch of the book about a whole family, not an individual or a couple — dinner, for a household with children still at home, is usually the last hour everyone still occupies at the same time. Paul's first attempt to fix it was a policy: phones surrendered at the table, non-negotiable. It lasted five days and produced, in his words, a war.
- What actually worked started by accident, in the kitchen, over a sprained wrist, and taught the whole family something about the difference between a checkpoint and a purpose. The twelve-year-old's punchline, delivered without even looking up from his plate, is the best line in the chapter and belongs to the book, not this summary.
