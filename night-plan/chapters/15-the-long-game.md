## Summary

- Marcus Hill found his evening structure in three weeks, then lost half of it to a single work trip — four nights in a hotel, a television across from the bed, none of his anchors in reach. The first time he fell off, it took six weeks to come back. The second time, three. By the time the book caught up with him, a day or two.
- The chapter separates implementation from maintenance — adopting a new behavior is a design problem, but surviving disruption is a resilience problem, and they need different tools entirely. The question was never whether Marcus would fall off. Everyone does. The question is how fast the return comes, and his restart is three moves, short and concrete enough to run on the worst night imaginable.
- The author steps out from behind his interview subjects here, for the first time at any length, to describe his own nights, at eighty-two, married fifty-six years. What he says there closes the chapter in his own voice rather than a subject's.
