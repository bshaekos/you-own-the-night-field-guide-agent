## Summary

- Sarah Okafor has tried everything — the app that locks your apps, the phone banished to another room, the digital sabbath, the productivity system with an evening reading block. She's a pediatric nurse who handles twelve-hour shifts with real discipline and judgment. None of it transferred to her own nights.
- The chapter lays out, as directly as anywhere in the book, why willpower loses and why subtraction fails. The apps are built and maintained by engineering teams of hundreds, iterating without pause on behavioral data from hundreds of millions of people. It is not a fair fight, and a rule that just removes something leaves a vacuum the mind fills with whatever's nearest — usually the very device just put down.
- What actually worked for Sarah wasn't a system. It was a handful of small changes that lowered the friction for what she wanted and raised it for what she didn't — and a single sentence of hers that's quoted more than anything else in the book.
