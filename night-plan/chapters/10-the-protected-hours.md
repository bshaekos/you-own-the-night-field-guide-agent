## Summary

- Nina Castellanos is a novelist who writes every morning between six and eight, and for years didn't understand that those two hours were downstream of what she'd fed her brain the night before. In her own phrase, she'd been tending the crop and neglecting the soil.
- Not a sleep book, the author says directly — there are whole bookshelves of those already — but one piece of the science matters enormously here: sleep is active synthesis, not passive recovery, and what's fed to it the evening before shows up, or doesn't, the next morning. Nina had an unusually precise instrument for measuring this. Most people don't, which is exactly why the cost goes unnoticed.
- The capacity being spent isn't the same for everyone — for Nina it was language, for others in the book it's something else entirely. The accounting is the same no matter the currency.

## The Four Questions

| Four Questions | |
|---|---|
| **Audit** | What work — creative, relational, professional — should your nights be serving? |
| **Cost** | What capacity gets depleted silently by your current habits: the patience, the focus, the warmth, the ear? |
| **Swap** | Decide, for one night, what you're willing to pay for tomorrow's morning, and what you're not. |
| **Check** | Did the morning the night paid for actually arrive? |
