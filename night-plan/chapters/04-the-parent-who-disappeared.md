## Summary

- Priya Shah is a pediatric dentist, a mother of two, and — by her own account — a parent who hadn't set her phone down during her children's bedtime in more than a year. She described it without drama, the way a doctor describes a chronic condition. What finally made her notice wasn't a book or a spouse. It was her nine-year-old son, mid-sentence, stopping to tell her plainly: you're not here.
- The chapter uses her evening to talk honestly about what it calls the sedative without rest — the particular numbness a depleted parent reaches for at the end of a fourteen-hour day, which asks for nothing and restores nothing. The part of Priya that could have chosen something else had already gone offline for the day.
- Her turning point wasn't about the phone at all. It was about the mornings, and about a version of herself who still had something left to give. The notecard itself — short, specific — is hers to teach the reader, in the chapter itself.
