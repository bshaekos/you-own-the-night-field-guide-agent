## Summary

- Tom Reyes had a theory about marriage: you measure it by the ordinary Tuesdays, not the big moments. By his own theory, his and Ellen's was in trouble — not from any single thing either of them could point to, but from two screens, three feet apart, on the same couch, every night.
- Their story introduces the twelve-second window: the interval engineers actually tested — six seconds too abrupt, twenty too long — before landing on the exact gap in which a tired adult can't quite gather the will to decline what's next. Not malice. Competence, aimed somewhere that happens not to be a marriage.
- What finally reached Tom wasn't a lecture. It was his brother, in one sentence, over a beer. What they built afterward was small, a little silly, and it held — the clearest early illustration in the book of the difference between willpower and a wall.
