## Summary

- Alex Rivera is twenty-six and has never lived a day without a feed. Not a figure of speech — structurally true. Those who came to the feed as immigrants remember another way, and can in principle go back to something. Alex has no "before" to return to. You cannot mourn what you don't know is missing.
- The door into Alex's evenings turns out not to be philosophy. It's the body. He's the most tired person in the book, with a specific, hard-to-locate tiredness that has nothing to do with overwork. For a young person caught in this pattern, the chapter argues, the useful entry point isn't meaning — it's the symptoms: the sleep, the headaches, the feeling of being oddly used up at the end of a day in which nothing demanding happened.
- Alex failed sixteen nights running at a flat instruction his doctor gave him, and then, on the seventeenth, something small shifted. What shifted, and what came back within weeks of it holding, is the chapter's turn.
