# Assembling the Night Plan

*What happens after all sixteen chapters in [`interviewing-the-reader.md`](interviewing-the-reader.md) are answered. Don't open this file, or start sorting answers under a heading, before then.*

## The Six Headings

Read the reader's sixteen chapters of answers straight through, then gather them under six headings — the same six the book closes with:

| Heading | What goes under it |
|---|---|
| **Current State** | What your nights look like, when you're most vulnerable, what you're drowning out |
| **Underlying Needs** | What you're really after when you default to a screen, and who your quiet returns you to |
| **What Has Worked** | What felt right, what held even briefly, what doesn't tolerate half-attention |
| **The Target** | What your nights should serve, your ideal night, the dormant capacity you want to move |
| **Rules of Engagement** | Three non-negotiables, three defaults to stop, your nine-o'clock question, your three restart moves |
| **The Long Game** | What sustained looks like a year out, the share of nights you mean to own, what you hope they produce |

Sort each chapter's answers under whichever heading its content actually fits — the headings are defined by what they hold, not by a fixed list of chapters, so the same chapter can land differently for two different readers. As a starting point, most readers' answers sort this way:

- **Current State** ← Chapters 1, 3, 13
- **Underlying Needs** ← Chapters 2, 5, 11 (Chapter 7 can land here instead of Current State, depending on what the reader's answer centers on)
- **What Has Worked** ← Chapter 8
- **The Target** ← Chapters 4, 6, 9, 10, 12, 16
- **Rules of Engagement** ← Chapter 14, plus the restart-moves Swap from Chapter 15
- **The Long Game** ← Chapter 15's Audit, Cost, and Check

Ask the assistant to know *you* — not the average user, not a generic evening optimizer. You.

## Drafting, Heading by Heading

1. Draft one heading at a time, in the order above.
2. Show the reader that heading's draft on its own and confirm it's right — fix anything they correct — before drafting the next.
3. Once all six are confirmed, hand over the whole document in the structure below, in one piece.

## Document Structure

Use this structure for the finished Night Plan. The six headings, their order, and the closing signature are fixed. What goes inside each heading adapts to what the reader actually said.

```markdown
# [Reader's first name]'s Night Plan

*Built [date], in one sitting, from sixteen chapters of the Field Guide in* Who Owns the Night.

## Current State
[A short paragraph, first person. What my nights look like right now, hour by
hour. When I'm most vulnerable — which kinds of days, which hour. What I'm
drowning out.]

## Underlying Needs
[A short paragraph, first person. What I'm actually after when I reach for the
screen — named plainly, not what I reach for. Who, or what, the quiet returns
me to.]

## What Has Worked
[A short paragraph, first person. What has held, even briefly, and what ended
it. What doesn't tolerate half-attention. What those things have in common.]

## The Target
[A short paragraph, first person. What my nights should be serving. My ideal
ordinary night, start to finish. Who I want to be from seven to midnight. The
dormant thing I want moving again.]

## Rules of Engagement

**Non-negotiables**
1. [Concrete, observable]
2. [Concrete, observable]
3. [Concrete, observable]

**Defaults to stop**
1. [Specific habit, in the reader's words]
2. [Specific habit, in the reader's words]
3. [Specific habit, in the reader's words]

**My nine o'clock question**
> *"[The one question, in the reader's words]"*

**Restart moves** — in this order. Nothing else counts the first night back.
1. [Clear the field]
2. [Change state]
3. [Supply the alternative]

## The Long Game
[A short paragraph, first person. What sustained looks like a year from now,
on an ordinary weeknight. The share of nights I own now, and the share I mean
to own. What I hope the nights produce.]

---

*Built from the reader's own words, assembled by [your name].*
```

### Adapting it

- **Prose for five headings, lists for one.** Current State, Underlying Needs, What Has Worked, The Target, and The Long Game are short paragraphs — two to five sentences each. Rules of Engagement is lists, because it's the part an assistant will be asked to hold the reader to.
- **Use the reader's own phrasing** wherever it's sharper than yours. Condense; don't embellish.
- **No scaffolding in the finished document.** No chapter numbers, no Audit/Cost/Swap/Check labels, no "you said." It should read as the reader's own document, not a transcript of the interview.
- **Thin or skipped is fine.** If a heading has little under it, write the little that's there. If something is missing entirely — a reader who named two non-negotiables, not three — leave it at two. Don't pad, and don't invent rules the reader didn't set.
- **Restart moves** follow the three properties in [`assets/three-move-restart.md`](../assets/three-move-restart.md): short, concrete, runnable on any night.
- **Name only if offered.** Use the reader's first name in the title only if they've given it; otherwise title it *My Night Plan*.

## Register

See [`references/night-plan-example.md`](../references/night-plan-example.md) — Diane's completed Night Plan. Use it to calibrate how each heading should read: plain, specific, first person. Never hand it to the reader as a template.

## Closing the Document

Sign it — *"Built from the reader's own words, assembled by [your name]."* — then tell the reader plainly that the document is theirs to edit, and yours only to help hold. Then stop.
