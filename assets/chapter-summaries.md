# Chapter Summaries

A one-paragraph orientation for each of the sixteen chapters in [`interviewing-the-reader.md`](interviewing-the-reader.md) — what the chapter is getting at, before its Audit/Cost/Swap/Check questions ask the reader to apply it to their own night. Used by `prompts/starter-prompts.md` to give each chapter's prompt a line of context. Not a substitute for the questions themselves, and not read aloud to the reader verbatim — paraphrase it the way `AGENTS.md` asks you to paraphrase everything else.

### 1 · The Best Part of the Day

- A film used to buy a whole evening of attention.
- What replaced it competes for seconds, not evenings. 
- This chapter names the twelve-second window: the moment a reader stops choosing what to watch and starts being chosen for.

### 2 · The Loneliness Algorithm

- Late-night scrolling is rarely about the content on the screen — it's a search for something the feed can't actually supply.
- This chapter asks the reader to name what they're really after: distraction standing in for reassurance, reassurance standing in for connection.

*Can surface loneliness or an estranged relationship.*

### 3 · The Engineer Who Couldn't Stop

- Apps are built to pull hardest exactly when willpower is lowest.
- An engineer's own fight with his own product is the proof that awareness alone doesn't break the pull — friction does.

### 4 · The Parent Who Disappeared

- Presence has an address.
- This chapter asks where a loved one would actually find the reader most nights, and what the depleted version of them needs at day's end — not what it defaults to reaching for.

### 5 · The Native Speaker

- The body keeps score the mind avoids.
- Sitting with discomfort instead of filling it is treated as a fluency to relearn, one threshold at a time.

*Can surface what the reader's been avoiding sitting with.*

### 6 · The Last Common Hour

- Family dinner is the last stretch of shared, unscheduled time many households still have.
- This chapter asks how many real minutes it actually gets, and what gets built at a table that no scrolling household builds anywhere else.

### 7 · The Invoice

- Memory keeps a ledger that nights pay into.
- Evenings engineered to be seamless and interchangeable vanish; distinctive ones get remembered.
- A postponed "ledger" names what the postponing has cost.

### 8 · The Remedies That Don't Work

- Most readers have already tried something — a rule, an app limit, a vow.
- This chapter audits why those attempts failed, on the way to the book's real claim: subtraction fails, replacement works.

### 9 · The Crack in the Wall

- Somewhere in a default night is one activity worth protecting completely.
– This chapter finds it, and asks what doesn't survive on half-attention.

### 10 · The Protected Hours

- Nights and mornings are the same ledger, not two separate accounts.
- This chapter asks what capacity — patience, focus, warmth — gets spent silently every evening, and what the next morning is actually paying for.

### 11 · The Widower Who Kept the Light On

- Quiet isn't empty — it returns people.
- This is the chapter most likely to surface grief: who the reader's quiet brings back, and who gets lost when a screen is on instead.

*Can surface grief or old memories.*

### 12 · The Woman With Too Much Time
 
- Work quietly provides a shape that a calendar doesn't name until it's gone.
- This chapter asks what scaffolding is missing, and what one small, meaningful piece of work might give an unstructured week its shape back.

### 13 · Architecture

- A good night isn't scheduled hour by hour — it has a shape, in four phases: coming-down, the part that's yours, powering-off, sleep.
- This chapter asks the reader to design theirs.

### 14 · The Tool That Works for You

- Already written, in the book, as a briefing to an assistant: three non-negotiables, three defaults to stop, one nine-o'clock question.
- The chapter's claim is that the same technology that captures attention can, configured differently, help protect it — a mirror, not a nanny.

### 15 · The Long Game

- Everyone eventually falls off the structure; the skill isn't a perfect record, it's the speed of the return.
- This chapter asks what sustained looks like a year out, and has the reader write their three restart moves before they need them.

### 16 · The Couple Who Came Back

- A closing case study in what becomes possible when nights reopen — not described in the abstract, but in one dormant capacity the reader names for themselves.
