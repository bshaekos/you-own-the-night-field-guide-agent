# Diane's Completed Night Plan

*An early reader, fifty-four, a hospital administrator in Ohio. Notice what it is not: not impressive, not literary, not optimized. It is specific, and specific is the entire job. Use it to calibrate register, never as a template to fill in.*

**CURRENT STATE.** I'm on the couch by 8:40 most nights with my phone and a glass of wine, and the news, and then the comments under the news. Worst nights are after days with conflict in them — a hard meeting, a complaint, anything with raised voices. Those nights I scroll like I'm looking for something, and I am, but it's not in the phone. What I'm drowning out: the replaying. I re-argue the day in my head and the phone turns the volume down on it.

**UNDERLYING NEEDS.** What I actually want at 8:40 is to feel off duty. Not entertained — released. The quiet returns me to my mother, who died three years ago and who I mostly only miss at night, and to my own thinking, which I used to like. I'd forgotten I used to like it.

**WHAT HAS WORKED.** The garden, two summers ago, until the weather ended it and I never restarted. Sunday calls with my sister — ninety minutes, never once regretted. Anything with my hands. Quilting, years back. What all of it has in common: I can't hold a phone while doing it, and I sleep afterward.

**THE TARGET.** Nights that end the day instead of leaking out of it. My ideal Tuesday: dinner, twenty minutes outside or with the quilt frame, call or read, lights lowering by ten. Who I want to be from seven to midnight: a woman who is finished, not a woman who is monitoring. The dormant thing I want moving again: the quilt. It's for my granddaughter. It's been a good intention for four years.

**RULES OF ENGAGEMENT.** Non-negotiables: phone charges in the kitchen at nine; Sunday is my sister's; the quilt frame stays set up in the den — visible, threaded, no putting it away. Stop-doing defaults: news after eight, comments ever, wine as the signal that the night has started. My nine o'clock question: *"Are you finishing the day, or re-arguing it?"* Restart moves, for after the bad stretches: kitchen charger, ten minutes at the frame, lights down at ten. Just those three. No apology tour.

**THE LONG GAME.** A year from now, sustained looks like five nights out of seven that I chose — not perfect nights, chosen ones. I currently own maybe two. What I hope the nights produce: the quilt, finished, on my granddaughter's bed, and a woman who goes up the stairs at ten-thirty with the day actually behind her.
